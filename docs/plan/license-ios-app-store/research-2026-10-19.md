# How close to the steering line can the license copy go? (checked 2026-10-19)

Research only. Nothing in the repo was edited. Repo facts are read from
`origin/feat/license-cosmetics-ios`; store facts are from the pages linked below, all fetched
2026-10-19 unless a row says otherwise.

## Bottom line

1. **Google Play can say a lot more than it does today, everywhere, with no program and no fee.**
   Google's consumption-only rule lets an app with no in-app purchase "provide additional
   information about purchasing options without direct links", and its own examples are
   imperatives such as "Head to our website to purchase more." So the `play` build can name the
   website, give a typeable address, and say "buy a license there", as long as nothing is tappable.
   This is the single big move, and it is low risk.
2. **No bucket should flip from key-only to a real link.** Every place that would allow a link
   without our own in-app purchase charges a fee and wants a program (Play US external content
   links, Play EEA/UK/Japan/Australia billing choice, Apple EU/Japan/Brazil), or demands an in-app
   payment option on the same screen (Apple EU, Japan, Brazil). Play US is the closest call and is
   still a no: the injunction stops Google from *banning* links, but Google's own program page says
   enrollment is required "prior to using external content links", and enrolling means fees
   (10–20% within 24 h) and the Play Billing Library.
3. **iOS outside the US is already at the edge.** Apple's 3.1.1(a) and 3.1.3 ban "buttons, external
   links, or other calls to action" toward non-IAP purchase in every non-US storefront, in the app
   *and its metadata*. Naming the website as a place to buy is a call to action. The current
   key-only copy is the most that is allowed; I only propose small wording changes that point to
   the key field, not to a seller.
4. **The real iOS risk is not steering, it is 3.1.1's license-key clause, and it applies in the US
   too.** Gating the icon picker on a key with no in-app purchase twin breaks the text of 3.1.1
   ("Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license
   keys") in every storefront. The US exemption covers links and buttons only; it does not waive
   in-app purchase. No 3.1.3 exception fits a notes app. I rate it **high** outside the US and
   **medium-high** in the US. Recommended mitigation is below.
5. **Nothing has passed App Review yet.** The live App Store version is 1.8.1 (released
   2026-10-10), and `v1.8.1` contains no license code at all. `v1.9.0` (tagged 2026-10-15) is the
   first tag with the license card and the US gate. Whatever happens to 1.9.0 in review is the first
   real evidence.

### Question 3 per bucket: may a key entered in the app enable the icon choice?

- **Play, every region: yes.** The `play` build sells nothing in the app, so it is a
  consumption-only app honoring access bought elsewhere (Help 10281818). Payments policy §2 lists
  "avatars" among items that must use Play Billing *when sold in the app*; we do not sell them in
  the app. Low risk.
- **iOS, every storefront including the US: not by the letter of the rules.** 3.1.1: "If you want
  to unlock features or functionality within your app ... you must use in-app purchase. Apps may
  not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys". The only
  escape for web-bought access is 3.1.3(b): "provided those items are also available as in-app
  purchases within the app". See the risk assessment below.

## Proposal per bucket

Vocabulary rules kept: no "paywall", "premium", "free", "donate"; "unlock(s)" only on link-out
builds; "included with a license" / "comes with a license" elsewhere. Placeholders use the
catalog's `{name}` grammar. The website address is a placeholder filled from Rust config, so it is
environment-split (staging vs production, the M3 rule) and never written into a translation.

### iOS US storefront — keep link-out, no copy change (risk: medium)

The steering side is clean (3.1.1(a) US exemption). The medium rating is the off-the-record report
that Apple wants IAP alongside a US link, plus the 3.1.1 key clause covered below. Keep Buy opening
in Safari/the default browser, never an in-app browser. No copy change.

### iOS every other storefront — stay key-only; reword to point at the key, never at a seller (risk: low for steering)

Do not name the website, FUTO's shop, a price, or "sold elsewhere" anywhere on these storefronts.
"Licenses are sold by FUTO" is still an encouragement to buy outside IAP under 3.1.3, so it is out.
The App Store product page is the same text for every storefront that shares a localization, so it
must not mention buying on the website either (3.1.1(a) says "apps and their metadata").

### Android `play`, US — do not flip to link-out (risk if flipped un-enrolled: high)

Reasons: (1) Google's program page requires enrollment before using links, and says nothing about
un-enrolled US links; (2) enrolling costs 10–20% per linked sale, reporting, refund duties and
the Play Billing Library, which breaks "no commission program"; (3) the `play` build has no way to
know the user is in the US without Play Billing's country lookup, and a wrong guess is a policy
violation outside the US; (4) the injunction is temporary (roughly to late 2027, unconfirmed) and
under active litigation. The "name the website" surface above gets most of the value with none of
this.

### Desktop and Android `direct` — unchanged link-out (risk: none from stores)

## Routing changes, in code terms (not written)

Today the decision is a boolean: `futo_notes_license::license_link_out(platform,
storefront_country, build_allows) -> bool` in `crates/futo-notes-license/src/config.rs:222`,
projected by `crates/futo-notes-ffi/src/license/contract.rs:144`, and fed to
`license_row_actions(status, link_out)`. Android passes `BuildConfig.LICENSE_LINK_OUT` (`true` for
`direct`, `false` for `play`, `apps/android/app/build.gradle.kts:110,115`); iOS passes
`buildAllows: true` and the StoreKit country from `LicenseStorefront.swift`.

The proposal needs a third state, so the boolean becomes an enum owned by Rust:

- `enum PurchaseSurface { LinkOut, NameWebsite, KeyOnly }` and
  `license_purchase_surface(platform, storefront_country, distribution) -> PurchaseSurface`:
  - Desktop → `LinkOut`.
  - iOS: storefront `Some("USA")` → `LinkOut`; anything else, including `None` → `KeyOnly`
    (fail closed, unchanged).
  - Android: `direct` → `LinkOut`; `play` → `NameWebsite` (worldwide; no country input, because
    the consumption-only rule is the same everywhere).
- The Android build flag changes from a boolean to a distribution value (e.g.
  `LICENSE_DISTRIBUTION = "direct" | "play"`), so Rust, not Gradle, decides what that means.
- `license_row_actions(status, surface)`:
  - `LinkOut`: unchanged (Buy/Renew, EnterKey, LostKey).
  - `NameWebsite`: EnterKey, LostKey. No Buy/Renew.
  - `KeyOnly`: EnterKey only (LostKey only if Justin takes the medium-risk option).
- `license_links` gains a display address (the typeable `{url}`), from the same environment-split
  config as `buy`. Shells render it as plain text only.
- Shell copy selection keys off the surface instead of "has Buy": card explanation
  (`license.explanation` / `license.websiteExplanation` / `license.keyOnlyExplanation`), headline
  (shown for `LinkOut` and `NameWebsite`), icon footnote (`footnote` / `footnoteWebsite` /
  `footnoteKeyOnly`) and sheet body (`sheetBody` / `sheetBodyWebsite` / `sheetBodyKeyOnly`).
- Tests to move: the `license_link_out` assertions in `contract.rs` (~line 871), Android
  `LicenseSurfaceTest` / `DistributionFlavorTest`, iOS `LicenseSurfaceTests`; add one asserting the
  `play` address is not clickable.
- Docs: `docs/spec/license.md` (the "Checkout availability" line ~350, the key-only vocabulary
  bullets ~175–183, § Store posture), a new ADR or an amendment to ADR-0005 recording "Play names
  the website", and marking plan D17/D23/D24 and the ADR-0007 Gap as deferred per today's no-IAP
  decision.

## The 3.1.1 key-unlock risk on iOS

**What the rules say.** 3.1.1 requires IAP to "unlock features or functionality" and names license
keys as a forbidden mechanism. 3.1.3 lists the only exceptions:

- 3.1.3(a) Reader apps: limited to "magazines, newspapers, books, audio, music, and video". A notes
  app is not one.
- 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform services: allows access to things bought on the web "provided those items
  are also available as in-app purchases within the app". We have no IAP, so it does not apply.
- 3.1.3(f) Free stand-alone apps: companions to a paid web tool for "VoIP, Cloud Storage, Email
  Services, or Web Hosting", and only with "no purchasing inside the app, or calls to action for
  purchase outside of the app". The license is not a web tool, and the US build has a Buy button.
- The US exemption in 3.1.1(a) covers "buttons, external links, or other calls to action". It
  does not remove the IAP requirement or the license-key sentence.

**Does a cosmetic differ from a feature?** Not in the text. 3.1.1 says "features or functionality"
and gives no cosmetic carve-out; I found no Apple text that treats non-functional items
differently. Apple's examples for IAP include things like in-game items, and Play's policy lists
"avatars" as in-app items explicitly. Choosing an app icon is a setting the user operates, so a
reviewer can call it functionality. I found no precedent either way for an icon picker gated by a
key; the icon-related rejections I found were about icons that could not be changed in the app
(guideline 4.6-style, Apple forum thread 751251; Twitterrific 2020), not about gating.

**Comparable apps.** Immich's mobile app accepts a license key bought at buy.immich.app, but its
license unlocks nothing ("there will never be any paywalled features"); it is a supporter badge.
That is the shape ADR-0005's "option A" relied on: a key that changes only the card. ADR-0006 moved
us off that shape by making the key enable icon choice. I did not find a non-reader app that
gates an in-app feature on a web-bought key with no IAP and is live on the App Store; one Apple
forum thread reports 11 rejections for exactly the 3.1.1 vs 3.1.3(b) argument.

**How it gets caught.** A reviewer with no key opens the icon picker, sees coin badges and
"included with a license", and finds no way to buy in the app (non-US) or a Buy button that leaves
the app with no IAP beside it (US). That is the textbook 3.1.1 rejection.

**Risk.** Non-US storefronts: **high**. US storefront: **medium-high** (the link itself is fine;
the key-gated feature without IAP is the same 3.1.1 problem). A rejection does not strike the
account, but a stable tag goes to Apple review with auto-release, so it stalls the whole iOS
release, including unrelated fixes.

**Recommended mitigation (pick one):**

1. **Recommended: do not gate the icon picker on iOS until an IAP exists.** Rust
   `license_includes` gains the surface or platform as input and answers "nothing gated" for iOS,
   so the iOS license again changes only the card (ADR-0005's option A, which the rules tolerate
   best). Android `play` and `direct` keep the gate. This reverses D14 ("every country, every
   platform") for iOS only, so it is Justin's decision. Zero 3.1.1 exposure.
2. **Keep the gate, contain the blast radius.** Submit the iOS gate in a version that carries
   nothing else urgent; put a review note that the key is bought on the web and that the icons are
   cosmetic; and pre-build option 1 behind the Rust input so a rejection costs one rebuild, not a
   redesign. Expect rejection outside the US.
3. **Gate only on the US storefront.** Narrows exposure to the medium-high bucket, but makes the
   same build behave differently by country for a feature, which is hard to explain to users and
   still not compliant on paper.

The existing escape hatch, a non-consumable IAP at the license price (ADR-0005 option B,
ADR-0007/0008), is the only fully compliant answer for a gated feature on iOS. It is deferred by
today's decision.

## Buried IAP (follow-up 2026-10-19)

Question from Justin: add an iOS in-app purchase (IAP) but keep it out of the way. Constraints:
no FUTOpay/lib-polar change (so the purchase is never turned into a key), no license sync.
The design is therefore a StoreKit 2 non-consumable that StoreKit verifies on the device and that
grants what a license includes on that Apple ID's iPhones and iPads only.

**Short answer: it works, and it is the cleanest way out of the 3.1.1 problem.** With an IAP in
the app, the key field becomes legal under 3.1.3(b) in every storefront. Outside the US, no rule
requires the IAP to be prominent, because the key field is not a purchase option and we show no
other way to buy. In the US there is one new limit. The Ninth Circuit (2025-12-11) let Apple stop
a developer's web button from being *more* prominent than Apple's IAP button. Apple's guideline
text does not use that power yet, so the US website button and the IAP button should be equal
weight. "Website filled, IAP as a grey row" is the one layout I would not ship. Overall risk:
**low outside the US, low-medium in the US.**

### 1. Must the IAP be equally prominent, on the same screen, or "alongside"?

It requires only that the IAP **exists** in the app. Nothing about prominence, placement or price. |
| All (whole guideline page) | A search of the full guidelines (fetched 2026-10-19, both halves of the page) found **no** sentence with "prominent" or "discoverable". | **EU / Japan / Brazil** (entitlement rules) | "Apple In-App Purchase must be displayed at least as prominently as any other payment option shown" and must be offered "on every screen where your app merchandises a digital good" (Apple's EU, Japan and Brazil payment-options pages). | **No.** These attach to the link entitlement, which we do not take. | Rest of world | 3.1.1(a) and 3.1.3 ban calls to action toward non-IAP purchase. | Unchanged. No website mention. The IAP is the only purchase option. |

**How far we can bury it:**

- **Outside the US:** the furthest safe spot is the **License card**: the key field stays the
  primary control, and a secondary, labeled **"Buy in the App Store"** button sits next to
  "I already paid", plus "Restore Purchases". The icon sheet can carry the same secondary button.
  Do not hide it further (no long-press, no buried sub-screen, no unlabeled control). The real risk
  is not a rule. It is the reviewer failing to find the purchase and sending an "Information
  Needed" request under 2.1. Developer-forum threads show this exact pattern: Apple asks for
  "steps for locating the in-app purchases", and one game was rejected for making the reviewer play
  before the purchase screen appeared (Apple forums [728321](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/728321),
  [817493](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/817493); secondary, developer reports). The fix
  is the App Review notes: "Settings › License › Buy in the App Store".
- **US:** "Buy on website" as a filled main button with IAP as a plain secondary row is exactly the
  layout the Ninth Circuit lets Apple forbid. Ship **two equal buttons** in the same style and
  size, side by side or stacked: "Buy on FUTO's website" and "Buy in the App Store". Equal weight is
  allowed with certainty. The repo spec rule that Buy is "the only filled button" (license.md
  ~line 616) has to change for the US card.

### 2. Rejections for a hard-to-find IAP, or for a higher in-app price

- **Hard to find:** found only 2.1 "Information Needed" requests and "could not locate the in-app
  purchases" rejections in developer forums (secondary; listed above). They are solved by review
  notes or by shortening the path. I found no rejection for an IAP that was visible but secondary.
- **Different price:** nothing in the guidelines requires the in-app price to match the web price.
  The only price rules are against misleading or "irrationally high" prices (3 intro, 2.3.1(a),
  5.6 "raise prices in a tricky manner"). The 3.1.1(a) entitlement text even lets a link say items
  "may be available for a comparatively lower price". Apple's 2021 *Cameron* settlement left
  developers setting their own prices (mjtsai summary, secondary). Higher prices through IAP than
  on the web have been common practice. Spotify and YouTube Premium are the well-known cases, but
  that is from general knowledge; I did not fetch a source. I found **no rejection for a higher
  IAP price**.
- **What we may *say* about the price:** US: we may say the website is cheaper. The injunction
  forbids Apple from limiting the "language" of US calls to action, apart from general content
  standards (Ninth Circuit item ii). Outside the US: we may not mention the website or its price at
  all (3.1.3). The repo rule "copy never states a price" makes this moot unless Justin lifts it.
  The StoreKit sheet shows the IAP price on its own.

### 3. What an IAP-only, StoreKit-verified entitlement implies

- **Restore is required.** 3.1.1: "you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any
  restorable in-app purchases." Apple's `AppStore.sync()` docs: "Include some mechanism in your
  app, such as a Restore Purchases button". The same page says reinstalls and new devices get
  transactions automatically through `Transaction.currentEntitlements`. So we need a
  **Restore Purchases** button that calls `AppStore.sync()` only on tap (it shows an Apple ID
  prompt), while the gate itself reads `currentEntitlements`.
- **Family Sharing is off unless we turn it on**, and it cannot be turned off afterwards ("After
  you enable Family Sharing for an Apple In-App Purchase, you can't turn it off",
  [Apple: Supporting Family Sharing](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/supporting-family-sharing-in-your-app)).
  Recommend leaving it off. That matches ADR-0007 and how the website key works, which is
  per-buyer.
- **Refunds handle themselves.** "Products that the App Store has refunded or revoked don't appear
  in the current entitlements" ([currentEntitlements](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction/currententitlements)).
  A refund removes the entitlement on the next check. Because the gate is on choosing, never on
  keeping (D2), the icon already set stays. Listen to `Transaction.updates` so a refund mid-session
  is picked up. "Refunds out of scope" (D18) needs no work.
- **Cross-platform: nothing forces it.** 3.1.3(b) lets web purchases work in the iOS app if an IAP
  exists. It says nothing about IAP purchases working on other platforms, and I found no rule that
  does. The obligations are on our copy instead:
  - The card must say where the purchase works, e.g. "Purchased in the App Store. Works on
    iPhone and iPad with this Apple Account."
  - No key is shown, because none exists.
  - No copy may promise desktop or Android. D28 already bans "your license is on all your
    devices".
  - A buyer who later wants Android or desktop pays again on the website. That is a product cost
    for Justin to accept, not a rule.
- **Other costs:**
  - The Account Holder must accept the Paid Apps Agreement and set up tax and banking in App Store
    Connect.
  - Apple takes 15% under the Small Business Program (from earlier research, not re-read today).
  - The product needs a review screenshot and goes into review with the binary.
  - StoreKit's on-device check (`VerificationResult.verified`) can be defeated on a jailbroken
    device. That is acceptable for a cosmetic.
  - I did not check whether the privacy label changes. We send nothing to our servers, so it
    probably does not.

### 4. Does adding an IAP change the steering picture?

- **US:**
  - The link stays allowed.
  - Adding an IAP gives Apple the prominence-parity lever from the Ninth Circuit (section 1).
  - It also settles the off-the-record report that Apple wants a US link "in addition to — but not
    as a replacement for" IAP: we would now have both.
  - The pending US commission on linked purchases applies whether or not we have an IAP. The
    district court's rate is still undecided, and the Supreme Court review (No. 25-1311) is on the
    contempt finding.
- **Non-US:** no change. The IAP does not unlock any mention of the website. The EU, Japan and
  Brazil entitlements stay unused, so their prominence rules do not apply.
- **App Store product page:** the IAP will be listed automatically under "In-App Purchases". The
  description still must not mention buying on the website (3.1.1(a) "and their metadata", and
  the Japan/Brazil pages ban it explicitly). A US-only localization could, but the listing text is
  shared across storefronts by language, so don't.
- **Play: no change.** The `play` build stays free of in-app purchases, so it stays a
  consumption-only app (Help 10281818) and keeps the "name pay.futo.org, no link" surface. Nothing
  about the iOS IAP should appear in Android copy.

### Refinement: an iOS-only purchase, labeled "no license key" (2026-10-19)

**(a) Scope: it must be "this Apple Account", not one device.** Apple's In-App Purchase page
(fetched 2026-10-19): "If someone redownloads your app or switches to a new device, be sure to
immediately provide access to the content or functionality from their past Apple In-App
Purchases. Use the currentEntitlements property ... Your app must also include a restore mechanism
for any restorable Apple In-App Purchases, per App Review Guideline 3.1.1." A non-consumable is
restorable by definition. Limiting it to one device would mean refusing a purchase that StoreKit
reports as owned on a new device, which breaks that sentence. So the honest scope is **"FUTO Notes
on iPhone and iPad signed in to the same Apple Account."**

Two more facts:

- **Mac.** If the iOS app is also available on Apple silicon Macs ("Designed for iPad", an App
  Store Connect setting), the purchase works there too, because it is the same app and the same
  account. I could not tell from the public App Store lookup whether that setting is on. Check it
  in App Store Connect, and either word the copy as "Apple devices" or switch Mac availability off.
- **Family Sharing.** It is **off by default**; the developer turns it on per product ("Developers
  can choose to turn on Family Sharing", and once on "you can't turn it off"). Recommend off.
  The copy then needs no family clause.

**(c) Does an iOS-only IAP count as the "same item" under 3.1.3(b)?** Yes, as far as the text
goes. The rule is about what the app gives access to: users may "access content, subscriptions, or
features they have acquired ... on other platforms or your web site ... provided those items are
also available as in-app purchases within the app." Inside the iOS app, the IAP and the web
license give the same thing: what a license includes (icon choice, the card). I found no
guideline, Apple page or reported rejection that requires an IAP to carry to other platforms, and
3.1.3(b) runs in one direction only (web → app). Nothing requires the same price either (section
2 above). Risk: **low.** The one thing that would break parity is the IAP granting *less inside
the app* than the key does. Keep the Rust truth table so `app_store: true` includes exactly what a
Licensed key includes.

**(b) Disclosure copy.** The limitation is a fact about the product being sold, so stating it is
honest disclosure, not a call to action. The line is crossed only when the copy points to where
else to buy. Outside the US it may say "no license key" and "not on other platforms"; it may not
say "website", "FUTO's store", "buy a license to use it everywhere", or anything with a price. In
the US it may add the website alternative, because the injunction forbids Apple from limiting the
"language" of US calls to action, apart from general content standards (Ninth Circuit, item ii).
Keep the US website line no more prominent than the App Store button (section 1).

Notes on the copy:

- Use "Apple Account". That is Apple's current name for it, as in the Family Sharing docs.
- Avoid "only on iOS". iPad runs iPadOS, and the Mac question above is open.
- "Other devices or platforms" is accurate only if Mac availability is off. Otherwise write "can't
  be used in FUTO Notes for Android, Windows, macOS or Linux". That list names platforms, not a
  store, so it is still not steering. It is a little more inviting, so I'd still call it low risk.
- The vocabulary rules hold: no "free", "premium", "paywall" or "donate". The App Store button
  says "Buy", not "unlock". "Included with a license" stays the outside-US wording elsewhere.
- Remaining unknown: whether a reviewer reads "doesn't come with a license key" as a hint to buy
  elsewhere outside the US. No rule says so and I found no rejection for disclosure of a purchase's
  limits. If Justin wants zero exposure, the outside-US text can drop the key clause: "Works in
  FUTO Notes on iPhone and iPad with your Apple Account."
