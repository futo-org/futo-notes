### 2. Rejections for a hard-to-find IAP, or for a higher in-app price

- **Hard to find:** found only 2.1 "Information Needed" requests and "could not locate the in-app
  purchases" rejections in developer forums (secondary; listed above). They are solved by review
  notes or by shortening the path. I found no rejection for an IAP that was visible but secondary.
- **Different price:** nothing in the guidelines requires the in-app price to match the web price.
  The only price rules are against misleading or "irrationally high" prices (3 intro, 2.3.1(a),
  5.6 "raise prices in a tricky manner"). The 3.1.1(a) entitlement text even lets a link say items
  "may be available for a comparatively lower price". Apple's 2021 *Cameron* settlement left
  developers setting their own prices (mjtsai summary, secondary). Higher prices through IAP than
  on the web have been common practice. Spotify and YouTube Premium are the well-known cases, but
  that is from general knowledge; I did not fetch a source. I found **no rejection for a higher
  IAP price**.
- **What we may *say* about the price:** US: we may say the website is cheaper. The injunction
  forbids Apple from limiting the "language" of US calls to action, apart from general content
  standards (Ninth Circuit item ii). Outside the US: we may not mention the website or its price at
  all (3.1.3). The repo rule "copy never states a price" makes this moot unless Justin lifts it.
  The StoreKit sheet shows the IAP price on its own.
