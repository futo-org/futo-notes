### Android text-first editor

- *(Android text-first editor)* Opening a note shows its title (from the filename) and the toolbar at once and no loading UI for the first 300 ms. A note still opening after that gets a thin (3 dp) indeterminate line under the title, over faint placeholder lines until any of its text shows; the line stays until the note can be edited. It overlays the text, so it comes and goes without moving anything, and with animations off (animator duration scale 0) it is a still segment. Its accessibility label is `editor.progressiveLoad.opening`. → apps/android/app/src/main/java/com/futo/notes/textfirst/TextFirstOpening.kt
- *(Android text-first editor)* A note longer than 16 KB first shows the screens parsed from its first 16 KB alone (quick open), read-only, while the whole open runs on the editor thread. The cut is the start of a top-level block at column 0 after an empty line, with that block's line inside the bytes read and the block after the cut parsed but not shown: the chunk rule above, applied to the Rust engine. Quick open declines (the note opens whole, as before) for a leading U+FEFF, front matter that does not close inside the read, any shown `[` when a `]:` appears anywhere after the cut (definitions resolve document-wide), and from a lone CR on; the engine's rows must also end at the cut. The first screens it shows are the whole open's byte for byte and style for style, so the handover keeps the view's text, spans and caret and streams the rest after them; should they ever differ, the whole open replaces them as before. → crates/futo-notes-editor/src/quick_open.rs, crates/futo-notes-editor/tests/quick_open.rs, crates/futo-notes-store/src/editor_session/quick_open.rs, apps/android/app/src/main/java/com/futo/notes/textfirst/EditorEngineStorage.kt
- *(Android text-first editor)* Until the whole open lands, scrolling and reading work, a tap places the caret (the keyboard reads it at once; the session takes it on landing, mapped through any edits before it), and typed keys and edit commands wait in order and are applied once after it, never early and never dropped. Toolbar edit buttons are disabled meanwhile, and a new width (a rotation) that cuts the first screens short is rendered again right after the handover. Nothing is written until the session exists. → apps/android/app/src/main/java/com/futo/notes/textfirst/EditorEngineOpening.kt, apps/android/app/src/main/java/com/futo/notes/textfirst/TextFirstToolbar.kt, apps/android/app/src/androidTest/java/com/futo/notes/textfirst/TextFirstProgressiveOpenTest.kt

- *(Android text-first editor)* A new width (rotation, split screen, a fold, a resized window) or text locale on a view holding more than 16,000 characters first cuts the view to a bounded prefix, then the engine streams the rest again in acknowledged slices: the main thread never lays out the whole note at once. The reader's line stays at the top (at once when it lies in the first screens, else when the stream reaches it); caret, selection and unsaved edits stay in the engine untouched. A shorter view re-lays out whole and also keeps the reader's line at the top. Other configuration changes (dark mode, a keyboard) keep the layout. Supplied 2,377,787-byte note on the debug emulator (2026-11-05): a rotation blocked the main thread ~4.4 s (262 skipped frames; an ANR on the second rotation, ~10 s on a Pixel 7a), now no frame over 81 ms. → apps/android/app/src/main/java/com/futo/notes/textfirst/TextFirstEditTextWidth.kt, apps/android/app/src/main/java/com/futo/notes/textfirst/TextFirstEditText.kt `onConfigurationChanged`, apps/android/app/src/androidTest/java/com/futo/notes/textfirst/TextFirstWidthChangeTest.kt
