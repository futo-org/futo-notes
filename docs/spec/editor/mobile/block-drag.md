# Block drag

Moving a block by hand in the native editor (#311). The engine decides what a press lifts, where
it may land and the move ([portable-core block movement](../portable-core/block-movement.md));
the lines here are the gesture that names the position under the finger and the slot it lands on.

A long-press lifts a block only while the keyboard is down; while the note is being edited every
long-press is the platform's (#441). The keyboard counts as up whenever a text field of the note's
screen holds focus, so a hardware keyboard typing into the note counts too. The web editor asked
only whether its body had focus; here the title and Find fields count as well.

> **Gap:** Android has no block drag gesture yet (#444): a long-press there is still the platform's.

- *(iOS)* With the keyboard down, holding a finger 340 ms within 10 pt anywhere on a line of the note body (its margins included) lifts what the engine says a press at the position under the finger lifts: on a photo, that photo's position; elsewhere, the character nearest the finger on that row, whole: right of a row's last character the press is on that character's start, never inside it (an emoji's two UTF-16 units). → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstDragFeel.swift `TextFirstLiftPolicy`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* While the keyboard is up (the body, an open table cell, the title or Find holds focus, the keyboard on screen or a hardware one) no press lifts and the long-press is the platform's as on a stock text view: the loupe, long-press selection, holding the caret to drag it, a selection's handles, dragging selected text, and a long-press on an image or a link. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `mayLift`, `armLift`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* With the keyboard down, a long-press that lifts nothing keeps the platform's (the loupe, the text menu with Paste): on a blank line, the note's empty last line after a final line ending, front matter, code nothing closes, and anywhere in an empty note. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `liftPlan`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* An empty task on the note's last line, which draws its box and no characters, lifts like any other line: its card and its dimmed place are its row. Dropped above it a block lands above the task, dropped below its row after it: two drop lines, at the top and the bottom of the row. The row is the one its box is drawn on, also where TextKit lays it out inside the row above (`- [ ] milk\n- [ ] \n- [ ] `): a press, the card and both drop lines use it, and a press on the row above lifts that row. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstLayoutFragments.swift `trailingRowFrame`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEmptyLastRowTests.swift → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `boundaryY`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* While a long note is still opening no press lifts: the long-press is the platform's everywhere in the note. The title field is not part of the body and keeps its own. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `mayLift`
- *(iOS)* With the keyboard down, a long-press on a photo that shares its Markdown line with other content lifts the photo alone and opens nothing: in "Nice!![](photo.jpg)" the photo lifts without "Nice!", and dropped just below (or above) its line it becomes a line of its own. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `liftPlan`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* A long-press on that line's text, or beside the photo on its row (off the photo), lifts the whole line, photo included. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `pressOffset`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* A long-press on a photo alone on its line, or in a quote or a table, lifts what its line lifts (on a table's grid, the whole table); one on a link lifts its line. Neither opens anything. → apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* With the keyboard down, the platform's long-presses wait for the lift and act only where it lifts nothing, and its text drag is off; a press that lifts starts neither the loupe nor long-press selection. Whether a press may lift is settled as its first finger touches down, so with the keyboard up nothing waits on the lift. A double-tap still selects a word, and a tap, a checkbox tap, vertical scrolling and a table's sideways scroll are unchanged. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `armLift`, apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+Taps.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A lift starts no editing: no caret, and the keyboard stays down through the drag and after the drop. At the lift an open edit menu closes and the finger stops scrolling the note until it lifts (the edge autoscroll below still scrolls it); after a move the selection stays with its text. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `beginBlockDrag`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* The lifted block rides a card, as the web editor drew it: the block drawn by the editor (the part that shows, for a block running off screen) about 4% larger than the block, with 14 pt corners, a hairline border, three shadows and the surface colour at 80% behind the block's opaque text; it fades in growing from 97% over 120 ms, follows the finger keeping the offset it had at the lift (as the web editor's, it is placed on screen at the lift and not held there after), and stays under it while the note scrolls. A lifted photo's card is the photo alone. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockCard.swift, apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockPreview.swift `blockCard`
- *(iOS)* A card is at most 40% of the screen's height; a taller block's card shows its top, the last 56 pt of the block's drawing fading into the card (the card's background, border and shadow do not fade). A capped card is placed so the finger is on it (40% of the way down when the block's own place would leave the finger off it) and inside what shows of the note, and the enlarged card keeps 8 pt from the screen's sides and from what shows. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockCard.swift `frame(for:finger:shown:screen:)`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockReflowUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* The note shows the move live while the block is carried (the web editor's reflow): the block is dimmed to 35% in its place until the gap first moves; from then on its place closes up, a gap opens where a release would land with a copy of the block in it at 35%, and the blocks between slide to make room. No drop line is drawn with the live preview. The note itself is not changed until the drop: the preview is a curtain over the visible note, painted with the editor's own surface colour (light and dark), holding slices of the note drawn by the editor's own drawing, so it shows exactly what the screen showed when it appears. The slices are drawn in idle time after the lift. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstReflow.swift, apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstSlicePainter.swift, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockReflowTests.swift
- *(iOS)* The gap is chosen from the finger's height only, starting from the gap open now: it moves past the stretch of note between it and the next place it may open (an engine slot, or the block's own place) once the finger passes that stretch's middle as it is drawn now (mid-slide included), and stays on the exact middle. Where the engine offers no slot (inside a quote, a code block, a table, a list that takes nothing) the finger has to pass the whole stretch up to the next slot. The block's own place means nothing moves. A stretch passed is drawn on the gap's other side, a block's height away, so a finger resting near a boundary between blocks of different heights does not make the gap flicker. iOS delivers a finger's moves once per frame, and the gap is judged once for each. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstReflowPlan.swift `gap(from:finger:)`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockReflowTests.swift
- *(iOS)* The blocks between slide over 0.18 s with the web editor's easing (cubic-bezier 0.2, 0.9, 0.3, 1), from wherever they are drawn mid-slide; a block whose drawing first shows is drawn in its place at once. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstReflow.swift `TextFirstReflowMotion`
- *(iOS)* A photo lifted out of a line of text (`Nice!![](a.jpg)`) previews like any block: its line closes up without it as the line will be laid out once the photo has left (laid out a second time, off screen, at the lift), and the photo sits in the gap as the line of its own it becomes (at the text's margin, its own height). Its place is its middle: the gap opens above or below its own line once the finger passes the middle of the part of the line above or below that. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstClosedLine.swift, apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockReflow.swift `reflowPlan`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockReflowTests.swift
- *(iOS)* The drag shows the drop line instead of the live preview with Reduce Motion on (nothing slides), and when the preview could not be built, which is logged with its reason; never because of a block's height or kind. With the drop line the block's place stays dimmed and the blocks around it do not move. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockPreview.swift `startPreview`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockReflowUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* At the lift the view asks the engine, in one call, only for the slots inside what TextKit has laid out (the screen and a margin), so a lift is as quick in a long note as in a short one. As the note scrolls it asks only for the stretch that newly comes near the screen, keeping the slots by their place in the text, never by where they were drawn at the lift. Every slot the engine offers is one where the drop will be accepted, so a drop line never promises a move that springs back. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `liftPlan`, apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockMove.swift `blockSlots`
- *(iOS)* With the drop line, it marks the engine's slot whose boundary is nearest the finger, among the slots that show; when one of the block's own two edges is nearer there is no drop line. A lifted photo has no such edges (every slot moves it, its own line's edges too): there is no drop line while the finger is nearer the photo's middle than any slot. A photo alone on its line lifts its line, and the card, the dimmed area and those two edges are the line's, even on the note's last line where the photo's range and its line's are the same; the view tells the two apart by whether what the engine returned runs from a line's start to a line's start. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `TextFirstDropTargets`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* With the live preview, a release with a gap open moves the block there as one Undo step. The card goes at once and the copy in the gap turns solid; the move is made at once under the curtain, then the slices and the copy slide the few points left to where the moved note draws them (a box, an indent or a number the block gained on arrival fades in), and the curtain comes down when the slides end, at most 0.4 s after the release, uncovering the same picture. The screen does not scroll at the drop. No block lifts until the curtain is down; a scroll, rotation or relayout while it settles takes it down at once. A release with the gap at the block's own place, or onto a move the engine refuses, changes nothing: the card goes and what slid slides back. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLanding.swift `landPreview`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockReflowTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockReflowUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* With the drop line, releasing over it moves the block there as one Undo step. Releasing with no drop line, or onto a move the engine refuses, changes nothing and the card slides back onto the block where it is drawn now (a photo above it may have loaded since the lift). → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrop.swift `endBlockDrag`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* Held within 64 pt of the top or bottom edge of what shows of the note, a lifted block scrolls the note: 200 pt/s at the zone's inner edge ramping to 1400 pt/s at the edge and beyond it, every frame while the finger holds still (a block lifted already inside a zone scrolls at once, and the speed follows the visible area when it changes under a still finger, as on rotation), a frame's step at most 0.05 s of travel, stopping at the note's ends and whenever the drag ends, however it ends. Where what shows is shorter than two zones the nearer edge wins. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTargets.swift `TextFirstAutoscroll`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragScrollTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* The live preview does not scroll: when the finger would start the autoscroll, what slid slides back to the note as it is (0.18 s), the curtain comes down with nothing jumping, and the drag goes on with the drop line, which then starts the scroll; it keeps the drop line until it ends. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockPreview.swift `handOver`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* While the note scrolls under a still finger the drop line follows the slot now nearest it, among the slots that scroll into view too, each read from TextKit's layout once the text is laid out. A drop line that changes slot because the note scrolled does not tick; only the finger's own movement does. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `dropTargets`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragScrollTests.swift
- *(iOS)* A block carried by the autoscroll lands where the drop line showed, upwards or downwards, and the note does not jump at the drop: when the block left from above the screen, the lines on screen stay where they were drawn and the block appears at the drop line, rather than everything moving up by the block's height. A block lifted on screen closes up its place as before. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrop.swift `heldLine`, `holdStill`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A change of layout alone during a drag (a photo finishing loading, a rotation) measures the drop line, the dim, the slots and the live preview again from where they are drawn now, and the drag carries on: the gap stays on its slot (or goes home if that slot no longer shows) and is judged again under the still finger, without a tick (a gap that opens so shows at once), and a slice drawn again starts from where the old one was drawn. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstTextView.swift `layoutSubviews`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragScrollTests.swift
- *(iOS)* A change to the note's text during a drag (a sync, an outside write, anything that moves the engine's revision) cancels it at once, with the finger still down. A sync that writes the note ends the drag as soon as the note's screen hears of it, before the editor has taken the new text in, and no block lifts or drops until it has: the card, the live preview, the drop line and the dim go at once, nothing moves, and there is no drop haptic. A text change while a drop is settling takes its curtain down at once. What the finger does until it lifts is not a drag. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `cancelBlockDrag`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A drag also ends with nothing moved when iOS cancels the touch, when the app stops being active (an incoming call, a switch to another app, going to the background), and when the note closes or leaves the screen (another note opened over it). → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `blockDragInterrupted`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragScrollTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A second finger on the note during a drag is ignored: it neither ends the drag nor moves the card, places no caret and scrolls nothing. → apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A debug build's puppet `state` reports a drag in progress as `blockDrag`: the display line lifted, how it shows the move (`preview`: `reflow` or `dropLine`), where a release lands (`lands`: the line it would land above, or the note's end; `dropLine` the same while the drop line shows), and what is drawn in screen points (the drop line's y, null while hidden; the card's frame; the scroll offset; with the live preview, `reflow`: whether its curtain shows and the frame of the copy in the gap). `previewUnavailable` gives the logged reason when the drag has no preview it should have. `landsY` is where the slot a release lands on is laid out now (screen y), and `lastLanding` how long the last drop's curtain stayed after the release. `dragPreview` is true while a preview or a settling drop covers the note. A debug build launched with `-FUTODebugReduceMotion` drags as with Reduce Motion on. → apps/ios/Sources/App/PuppetNative/PuppetNativeEditor.swift
- *(iOS)* A block dropped into a list arrives as the engine writes it ([block arrival](../portable-core/block-arrival.md)): a plain line or a photo dropped into a checklist becomes an unchecked task, a bullet dropped into a numbered list takes its number and the items below count on, a nested item dropped among top-level items comes out to their level, and an item dropped outside any list goes to the left margin. The card, the dimmed place and the copy in the gap show the block as it looked where it was lifted; the changes appear at the drop (fading in as the copy settles), in the same Undo step. → crates/futo-notes-editor/src/engine/commands/lists/arrival.rs, crates/futo-notes-editor/src/engine/tests/moves/arrival.rs
- *(iOS)* Haptics, as the web editor's: a medium impact at the lift, a selection tick each time the finger moves the drawn gap, or carries the drop line, to a slot it was not on (none while it stays, none for the block's own place, none when the layout or the scroll changes under a still finger), and a light impact only when a move is made. A release that moves nothing is silent. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `TextFirstDragHaptics`, `TextFirstBlockDrag.follow`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
