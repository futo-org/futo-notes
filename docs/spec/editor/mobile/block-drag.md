# Block drag

Moving a block by hand in the native editor (#311). The engine decides what a press lifts, where
it may land and the move ([portable-core block movement](../portable-core/block-movement.md));
the lines here are the gesture that names the position under the finger and the slot it lands on.

A long-press lifts a block only while the keyboard is down; while the note is being edited every
long-press is the platform's (#441). The keyboard counts as up whenever a text field of the note's
screen holds focus, so a hardware keyboard typing into the note counts too. The web editor asked
only whether its body had focus; here the title and Find fields count as well.

> **Gap:** Android has no block drag gesture yet (#444): a long-press there is still the platform's.

- *(iOS)* With the keyboard down, holding a finger 340 ms within 10 pt anywhere on a line of the note body (its margins included) lifts what the engine says a press at the position under the finger lifts: on a photo, that photo's position; elsewhere, the character nearest the finger on that row, whole: right of a row's last character the press is on that character's start, never inside it (an emoji's two UTF-16 units). → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `TextFirstLiftPolicy`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* While the keyboard is up (the body, an open table cell, the title or Find holds focus, the keyboard on screen or a hardware one) no press lifts and the long-press is the platform's as on a stock text view: the loupe, long-press selection, holding the caret to drag it, a selection's handles, dragging selected text, and a long-press on an image or a link. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `mayLift`, `armLift`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* With the keyboard down, a long-press that lifts nothing keeps the platform's (the loupe, the text menu with Paste): on a blank line, the note's empty last line after a final line ending, front matter, code nothing closes, and anywhere in an empty note. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `liftPlan`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* An empty task on the note's last line, which draws its box and no characters, lifts like any other line: its card and its dimmed place are its row. Dropped above it a block lands above the task, dropped below its row after it: two drop lines, at the top and the bottom of the row. The row is the one its box is drawn on, also where TextKit lays it out inside the row above (`- [ ] milk\n- [ ] \n- [ ] `): a press, the card and both drop lines use it, and a press on the row above lifts that row. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstLayoutFragments.swift `trailingRowFrame`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEmptyLastRowTests.swift → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `boundaryY`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* While a long note is still opening no press lifts: the long-press is the platform's everywhere in the note. The title field is not part of the body and keeps its own. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `mayLift`
- *(iOS)* With the keyboard down, a long-press on a photo that shares its Markdown line with other content lifts the photo alone and opens nothing: in "Nice!![](photo.jpg)" the photo lifts without "Nice!", and dropped just below (or above) its line it becomes a line of its own. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `liftPlan`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* A long-press on that line's text, or beside the photo on its row (off the photo), lifts the whole line, photo included. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `pressOffset`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* A long-press on a photo alone on its line, or in a quote or a table, lifts what its line lifts (on a table's grid, the whole table); one on a link lifts its line. Neither opens anything. → apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* With the keyboard down, the platform's long-presses wait for the lift and act only where it lifts nothing, and its text drag is off; a press that lifts starts neither the loupe nor long-press selection. Whether a press may lift is settled as its first finger touches down, so with the keyboard up nothing waits on the lift. A double-tap still selects a word, and a tap, a checkbox tap, vertical scrolling and a table's sideways scroll are unchanged. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `armLift`, apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+Taps.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A lift starts no editing: no caret, and the keyboard stays down through the drag and after the drop. At the lift an open edit menu closes and the finger stops scrolling the note until it lifts (the edge autoscroll below still scrolls it); after a move the selection stays with its text. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `beginBlockDrag`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* The lifted block is a card, a snapshot of the block at its full on-screen size (the part that shows, for a block running off screen) with a see-through background, that follows the finger and stays under it while the note scrolls; the block's place in the note is dimmed, and the dim scrolls with the note and covers whatever part of the block shows. A lifted photo's card, and the dimmed place, are the photo alone. Neighbouring blocks do not move. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `TextFirstBlockDrag`
- *(iOS)* At the lift the view asks the engine, in one call, only for the slots inside what TextKit has laid out (the screen and a margin), so a lift is as quick in a long note as in a short one. As the note scrolls it asks only for the stretch that newly comes near the screen, keeping the slots by their place in the text, never by where they were drawn at the lift. Every slot the engine offers is one where the drop will be accepted, so a drop line never promises a move that springs back. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `liftPlan`, apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockMove.swift `blockSlots`
- *(iOS)* A drop line marks the engine's slot whose boundary is nearest the finger, among the slots that show; when one of the block's own two edges is nearer there is no drop line. A lifted photo has no such edges (every slot moves it, its own line's edges too): there is no drop line while the finger is nearer the photo's middle than any slot. A photo alone on its line lifts its line, and the card, the dimmed area and those two edges are the line's, even on the note's last line where the photo's range and its line's are the same; the view tells the two apart by whether what the engine returned runs from a line's start to a line's start. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `TextFirstDropTargets`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
- *(iOS)* Releasing over a drop line moves the block there as one Undo step. Releasing with no drop line, or onto a move the engine refuses, changes nothing and the card slides back onto the block where it is drawn now (a photo above it may have loaded since the lift). → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrop.swift `endBlockDrag`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* Held within 64 pt of the top or bottom edge of what shows of the note, a lifted block scrolls the note: 200 pt/s at the zone's inner edge ramping to 1400 pt/s at the edge and beyond it, every frame while the finger holds still (a block lifted already inside a zone scrolls at once, and the speed follows the visible area when it changes under a still finger, as on rotation), a frame's step at most 0.05 s of travel, stopping at the note's ends and whenever the drag ends, however it ends. Where what shows is shorter than two zones the nearer edge wins. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTargets.swift `TextFirstAutoscroll`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragScrollTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* While the note scrolls under a still finger the drop line follows the slot now nearest it, among the slots that scroll into view too, each read from TextKit's layout once the text is laid out. A drop line that changes slot because the note scrolled does not tick; only the finger's own movement does. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockLift.swift `dropTargets`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragScrollTests.swift
- *(iOS)* A block carried by the autoscroll lands where the drop line showed, upwards or downwards, and the note does not jump at the drop: when the block left from above the screen, the lines on screen stay where they were drawn and the block appears at the drop line, rather than everything moving up by the block's height. A block lifted on screen closes up its place as before. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrop.swift `heldLine`, `holdStill`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A change of layout alone during a drag (a photo finishing loading, a rotation) measures the drop line, the dim and the slots again from where they are drawn now, and the drag carries on. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstTextView.swift `layoutSubviews`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragScrollTests.swift
- *(iOS)* A change to the note's text during a drag (a sync, an outside write, anything that moves the engine's revision) cancels it at once, with the finger still down. A sync that writes the note ends the drag as soon as the note's screen hears of it, before the editor has taken the new text in, and no block lifts or drops until it has: the card, the drop line and the dim go, nothing moves, and there is no drop haptic. What the finger does until it lifts is not a drag. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `cancelBlockDrag`, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A drag also ends with nothing moved when iOS cancels the touch, when the app stops being active (an incoming call, a switch to another app, going to the background), and when the note closes or leaves the screen (another note opened over it). → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `blockDragInterrupted`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragScrollTests.swift, apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A second finger on the note during a drag is ignored: it neither ends the drag nor moves the card, places no caret and scrolls nothing. → apps/ios/UITests/TextFirstBlockDragAutoscrollUITests.swift
- *(iOS)* A debug build's puppet `state` reports a drag in progress as `blockDrag`: the display line lifted, what its drop line shows (the line it would land above, or the note's end), and what is drawn in screen points (the drop line's y, null while hidden; the card's frame; the scroll offset). → apps/ios/Sources/App/PuppetNative/PuppetNativeEditor.swift
- *(iOS)* A block dropped into a list arrives as the engine writes it ([block arrival](../portable-core/block-arrival.md)): a plain line or a photo dropped into a checklist becomes an unchecked task, a bullet dropped into a numbered list takes its number and the items below count on, a nested item dropped among top-level items comes out to their level, and an item dropped outside any list goes to the left margin. The card and the dimmed place show the block as it looked where it was lifted; the changes appear at the drop, in the same Undo step. → crates/futo-notes-editor/src/engine/commands/lists/arrival.rs, crates/futo-notes-editor/src/engine/tests/moves/arrival.rs
- *(iOS)* Haptics, as the web editor's: a medium impact at the lift, a selection tick each time the finger carries the drop line to a slot it was not on (none while it stays, none for hiding over the block itself, none when the note scrolls under a still finger), and a light impact when the block lands. A release that moves nothing returns the card silently. → apps/ios/Sources/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstEditorController+BlockDrag.swift `TextFirstDragHaptics`, `TextFirstBlockDrag.follow`, apps/ios/Tests/Editor/TextFirst/TextFirstBlockDragTests.swift
